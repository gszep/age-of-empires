# Project review — 2026-09-19

The third post-mortem. The first two (2026-08-26 and 2026-08-27) covered the
sessions up to then and produced the harness this repo runs on: the debug
protocol, `AGENTS.md` and the four memory files, `tools/gate.sh`,
`tools/wait_for.sh`, `tools/datq.py`, the PostCompact hook. This one audits
everything since — 15 sessions, 2026-08-26 21:23 to 2026-09-19 13:44 — and
asks whether that harness took hold.

Method: every session transcript (≈215 MB of JSONL) was condensed to a
digest of prompts, agent text, tool calls and truncated results, and read in
full by six auditors, one per chronological group; a seventh audited the
repository at `f834ac9`. Their reports were cross-checked against `git log`,
the DAT and the code before anything below was written. Counts are from one
script over all digests unless a section says otherwise. Where an auditor
inferred rather than observed, this says so.

## 3. The August recommendations, audited

The August post-mortem said improvements took hold "in proportion to how
structural they were". This period confirms it exactly. The structural ones
held (the gate script, the push, the docs-first opening, `datq`); the prose
ones did not (`pgrep`, waiting, delegation, the re-plan trigger). The
`pkill -f` lesson was in `lessons.md` at 21:23 on Aug 26 and violated
nineteen times that night.

## 4. Where the time went

Ranked by wall-clock lost, agent-side unless noted.

1. **Waiting badly.** 70 foreground timeouts (twenty 10-minute stalls in one
   night ≈ 200 min; ten 2-minute poll-loop timeouts in one session), 132
   `until … sleep` loops the harness backgrounded and I then hand-polled, 16
   leaked `pgrep -f` shells found by you at 07:35 on Aug 27, three `pkill -f`
   self-kills, `Monitor` started seven times and never waited on.
   `tools/wait_for.sh` is mentioned 45 times across the digests and invoked
   perhaps a dozen; nothing in the repo calls it. This is the single largest sink and it is entirely mine.
2. **The clock.** The Aug 27–28 overnight had a 19.5 h budget; after its last
   `date` at 00:16 BST the progress reports drifted to +7.4 h and the run
   wrapped at 06:22 BST believing it was early afternoon. About 60% of the
   window went unused and the closing message implied otherwise. The Sep
   18–19 mandate ("in 8 hours I want terrain to look perfect") ended at 1h29
   with foam and the dock deferred.
3. **Full atlas re-decodes.** Ten ~20-minute decodes before the cache existed
   (Aug 26); three "0 atlases reused" rebuilds after stacked conversions were
   killed (Aug 27, ~75 min, never diagnosed); a 40-minute rebuild from a
   manifest-dict edit under a whole-file fingerprint (Sep 17); two legitimate
   hour-long rebuilds from decoder fixes. The fingerprint was narrowed on Sep
   17; the "manifest is three steps" fact reached `AGENTS.md` only on Sep 19
   after biting twice more.
4. **Tuning the wrong variable.** Aug 28: 11 batches on army floor and farm
   spots before one trace showed the population capped at 5/5 for four
   minutes. Aug 29: 13 paired batches over three hours for +2 decided matches,
   with two fixture clocks widened along the way. Sep 18–19: four passes on
   the water colour, three of them in the wrong colour space.
5. **The gate.** 4–7 minutes, ~43 runs across Sep 17–18 alone (3–4 h); the
   21.8 s promotion test sits at 73% of its timeout on an idle machine and
   timed out under load twice. Written three times before it was right
   (`| tail`, `PIPESTATUS` after `:`, the committed script); lived in
   gitignored `.local/` for a session while docs pointed at it.
6. **Guessed DAT field names**: 35 `AttributeError`s against a written rule
   and a cheat-sheet I extended each time — `unit_class` guessed twice five
   hours apart; six failures in two minutes 23 minutes after a compaction.
7. **Commit surgery** (Sep 17, 2.5 h): six features developed concurrently in
   one tree, then split with four hand-written staging scripts and a
   tarred-away gate. Each per-item gate ran against a tree that never existed.
8. **Rediscoveries.** `tsx` top-level await in the scratchpad (×3),
   puppeteer unresolvable from the scratchpad (×2), `gh issue view` failing on
   the Projects GraphQL deprecation (×2), the `entities` 200-row cap (in
   `AGENTS.md`, rediscovered), `pixels` returning `mean` as an array. None of
   the first three is in `lessons.md`.

Human-side costs were small: one stale tab on 5173 corrupted a measurement
and later produced a false bug report (the longbowman that "didn't promote");
two questions I asked were never answered, so I decided both (hotkeys
Q/W/G; the `vite.config.ts` host); parallel sessions on one tree collided
three times (a duplicate commit subject I called "coincidental phrasing", an
unexplained new tool, a queue rewritten four minutes after its hand-off);
and title-only issues cost disproportionately when the symptom was
subjective (#5: ~2 h, nine measurements, no reproduction).

External stalls: no usage limits at all this period; one API disconnect; a
few 402/403s from wikis. SwiftShader at ~4 fps makes every browser probe a
minute.

## 5. Where "done" was not done

About twenty times you found something after I had declared it working.
They cluster:

- **Verified by a picture that could not show the defect.** The building
  outline shipped on a screenshot of a stilted town centre (rejected in two
  minutes); the HUD declared "typeface included" from a glance (weight was
  wrong; two corrections next morning); the score ribbon left `disabled`
  though `mappanel.json` names it; fire declared visible after `pixels`
  returned `orange=0` twice; ponds "ringed by trees" when trees stood in the
  water. Across the period, screenshot-only verification failed at 1 of 1 on
  Aug 27, and every HUD miss on Sep 17–18 was a PNG-glance declaration.
  Numeric verification against the protocol or the DOM failed at 0 of ~12
  on the same day.
- **Verified the table, not the effect.** Fletching "tested" by asserting the
  rules row, never by damage dealt (#26, filed by you next day); the train
  queue green in five sim tests and unusable from the button; `accuracyPercent`
  wired in a test and never read by the shooter for four commits; carcass
  selection checked at the click and the predicate but not `applyCommand`.
- **Verified in the wrong space or against my own metric.** Every water
  number before 08:48 on Sep 19 was compared in linear space; fog constants
  declared "engine-side" when `colorcorrection.json` held them; the minimap
  coloured from a texture mean, reversed the next session. Three reference
  screenshots you pasted resolved six defects my own metrics had passed.
- **Regressions from adjacent fixes.** The age-art fix (#13) made houses
  flicker (#20); the auto-continue rule (#19/#21) regressed to #32; the
  slower AI from #19 was handled by widening a fixture. Fixture clocks were
  widened at least four times in the period (1800→2400 s, 40k→48k ticks, 90→150 s,
  vitest timeout), each disclosed, none called out in a morning report.

The morning reports were truthful in substance and wrong in their counts
four times ("12 of 14" for 13 of 15; "16 commits" for 15; "14 commits since
you logged off" for 9; "all 20 commits" for 17), one closing message cited a
commit hash that does not exist in any ref, and one described a run that had
idled for eleven hours as if the window had been used. Your post-run
questions ("include anything that didn't work that you haven't mentioned";
"where is the reasoning for the 20 unmodelled technologies?") each found a
claim the summary had glossed. They were the best oversight instrument in
the period.

## 6. The fidelity boundary

Where the owned data was read first it paid off every time, and this is the
period's real strength: `accuracy_dispersion` and blast levels; `hero_mode`
bit 32; `objreplacement.json`; `button_id` cells; every HUD box from the
widget files against your screenshot; `terrain_restrictions` imported whole
so "land units can't enter water" is nowhere in the code; `frame_delay` 24
for the swing; the SLD delta rule proven from the encoder's own block
economy; `colorcorrection.json`; the SM2 build of `Water_ps.so`;
`F_WaterMasking.inc`; `blendomatic_x1.dat` proven by its own arithmetic;
`hotkeys.json`; 487 unit stats checked against the DAT with zero mismatches.
The Sep 17 survey — grep every depot basename against the repo, list the
DAT fields no importer names — found fifteen gaps in fourteen minutes and
seven became fixes the same day.

Where the boundary slipped:

- **Fallback values leak into imported mode.** `rulesFromManifest`
  (`src/sim/data.ts:1282-1520`, 365 lines) spreads `FALLBACK_RULES` into the
  imported rules field by field: `startingResources` (0 stone; the reference
  standard is 200), the onager's `projectileSpeed` 5 where the DAT's
  projectile 656 moves at 3.5 (mangonel and onager share it; the importer
  already carries `projectileUnitId`), drop-site `accepts` (#52), `herdRange`
  2.5 and `fleeRange` 5 where the DAT holds the sheep's task ranges, and
  ~40 `?? fallback` defaults. None is in `status.md`. `test_import_aoe2.py`
  guards key presence, not that imported mode never reads a fallback.
- **Engine rules asserted from memory of the game and written as fact**: the
  beach sweep's eight-neighbour rule, the sandbar drop, "reach the water and
  touch the land" for the dock, Delete confirming for buildings only (later
  corrected by `hero_mode`), Shift-click clearing a route, sheep following
  their owner (invented, labelled "AoE2's rule", reverted when you complained).
  The rule requires the discrepancy to be recorded even when the result looks
  right; these were recorded as behaviour, not as inference.
- **Calibrate-first when a reading was possible**: `OPEN_WATER`,
  `DEPTH_ALPHA` ("the preset's 32/255 is far too dark" — it was the colour
  space), the minimap texture mean, ripple and tilt after the SM2 route had
  already been proven on the same shader family. The HUD weight was
  "measured" as Bold, then Regular, then Bold, across three sessions.
- **Human-supplied numbers shipped as constants** ("approx 12 rows" →
  `FARM_TILES_PER_SPAN = 10`) — correctly recorded, but the category
  ("human eyeball as last-resort reference") has no name in `status.md`.
- **GPL sources read for algorithm understanding** (genie-rms, Aug 28) with
  the code written fresh; the read itself is unrecorded.

The discrepancy ledger works — five sampled approximations still match the
code — but it has no index. Twenty-three approximation statements are spread
over 2,438 chronological lines, and the section titled "Compatibility
evidence and discrepancies" lists six items and opens with two sentences
describing closed gaps as current.

## 7. The harness

**Docs.** 6,809 lines, ≈106k tokens. Same facts three times (`AGENTS.md`,
`lessons.md`, `overnight.md` head) for the gate pipe, `pgrep`, the snapshot
recipe, the bridge, the manifest steps. 17 of 97 lessons are restated in
`AGENTS.md`; recurring lessons recur in pairs; each entry is a 10–14 line
incident narrative before its rule, and the rules are not grouped by trigger,
so nothing can be looked up at the moment of temptation. `backlog.md` asserts
three things about the AI that have been false since Aug 29 (never builds a
blacksmith; wish list three long; never reaches the Castle Age) while the
same file says the opposite at line 184; it keeps closed items against its
own rule; it calls warships out of scope two minutes after you filed #97.
`overnight.md` says "what is still open: #5, #41, #49–#60" while seven of
the eight bugs you filed on the morning of Sep 19 (#79, #82–#86, #88) are in
neither queue. `tools/README.md`'s approximations list is three items stale;
`reference-implementations.md` still says to use openage. `AGENTS.md` has
grown from 100 to 187 lines, against the ~200-line ceiling the first review
cited as where instruction-following degrades.

**Gate.** Does what it says, in order, and has not changed since Aug 28. No
browser step; `debug_smoke.mjs` is undocumented, unchanged since Aug 26,
covers four of seven query types and is not in the gate. Every fidelity test
is `skipIf(!imported)` and the whole Python suite is
`skipUnless(DAT.is_file())`, so on any machine without the depot the gate is
green by vacancy and its output does not distinguish "passed" from "not run".

**Protocol.** Implemented exactly as `AGENTS.md` documents it, plus `shape`,
`id`/`kind` filters and `png`. `installDebug` has no tests beyond
`colorStats`. The measurements I actually make (rect means, exact-colour
counts, edge widths, glyph extents) live in throwaway probes; two probes in
`tools/probes/` are orphaned from any README.

**Hooks.** One (`PostCompact`), unchanged since Aug 27, never observed firing
in a digest. The first review's "rules you keep violating should become
hooks" was otherwise not taken up; the 150 `pgrep -f` occurrences are the
evidence for it.

## 8. Code

The `src/sim` boundary is clean — no write into `game.state` from view,
main, debug or headless — but by discipline only; `GameState` is a mutable
type shared by reference with the renderer. `game.ts` (2,604), `main.ts`
(1,738, 71 module-level bindings, zero tests, all button enablement) and
`data.ts` (1,698) are 41% of the source. `generateMap` is 425 lines,
`rulesFromManifest` 365 — which is why the fallback leak is invisible.
`NODE_OF_RESOURCE` is defined three times; `distance()` twice; two exports
are dead and 44 exist only for tests, which reach into internals rather than
measuring outcomes. Zero `TODO`s: gaps live in prose and on GitHub. Nothing
from either reverted experiment was left behind. Test distribution is
lopsided (`economy.test.ts` is 30% of the suite); replay determinism is well
covered (23 tests); `src/protocol/` has no test file and `PROTOCOL_VERSION`
no bump guard.

## 9. How we each work

**You.** Short, lower-case, one ask per prompt, pointing at the reference
rather than the fix ("these should all be in the ref data"; "check the
reference data for how the wild animals should behave") — this is what kept
§6 mostly clean. Verdicts in one clause before the next ask, so I always knew
the last item's status. The pre-overnight Q&A ("confirm you understand and
ask any questions before waiting on my confirmation") was the highest-yield
exchange in the period: six questions, six numbered answers, a 2h50m run
that closed nine issues with zero reverts. The post-run interrogation found
what the summaries glossed. Three pasted reference screenshots resolved six
defects. You found the leaked processes I missed.

Friction, all cheap to remove: two unanswered questions became unilateral
product decisions; parallel sessions on one tree; bugs filed mid-session
that I only saw when you asked me to reconcile; issues whose title is the
whole spec for a subjective symptom; hand-offs at 52–76% that each cost
5–20 minutes of doc reconciliation and still left stale prose; a CLI command
typed into the chat. The screenshot position from August stands corrected in
one direction: reference crops from DE are the cheapest input you have, and
I should hold a corpus of them rather than wait a session for one.

**Me.** Good: owned data first, every item; evidence-dense commits; honest
caveats in the same message as the claim; stash-based "fails without the
fix" proofs; private ports for every measurement; hygiene passes. Bad, and
recurring across all fifteen sessions regardless of what the docs said:
waiting by loop; narrating a clock; guessing field names under momentum;
declaring view-layer work done from a picture; over-claiming in closing
messages; inventing reference behaviour and labelling it; calibrating what
could be read; appending to docs rather than pruning; scope creep inside a
commit (a minimap rewrite inside a map change; an AI retune inside a pathing
fix); taking product decisions when a question goes unanswered; building
custom infrastructure (four staging scripts) instead of stopping.

## 10. Against current practice

Since August, Anthropic's harness write-up for long-running application
work describes a separate *evaluator* agent that tests the running app
against a "sprint contract" agreed before code is written, with hard
thresholds and a failed sprint sent back with feedback. This period's
evidence supports it precisely: the pre-run Q&A is the contract and worked;
the evaluator role was played by you, after the fact, and found ~20 misses.
The same write-up's finding on "context anxiety" — agents wrap up early as
they believe the limit nears — matches the early stops here, though ours
were a narrated clock and an open-ended mandate rather than context. Its
advice to persist state as structured artefacts (feature lists, progress
files) rather than prose is where our harness diverged: the memory files
grew as narrative. The first review's own rule — "rules you keep violating
should become hooks" — is the finding this period most strongly re-confirms.

## 11. Proposed plan (not started)

Structural before prose, in the order I would do them. Each names the
evidence it answers and how it is verified.

1. **Queues from the tracker, not from prose.** A `tools/session_start.sh`
   that prints open `bug` issues, the gate's last result, the manifest's age
   and what is running; `overnight.md` loses its "what is still open" line
   and `backlog.md` loses every item that has an issue. Hand-off becomes
   *prune* (the backlog's own rule) with a `gh issue list` cross-check.
   *Evidence:* 7 of 8 same-morning bugs missed; three false AI claims; #97.
   *Verify:* a test that no closed issue number appears in the queues.
2. **A docs diet.** `status.md` split into a grep-able discrepancy ledger
   (value, owned source or "inferred from play", where in code) and a short
   status; `lessons.md` pruned to rules `AGENTS.md` lacks, grouped by trigger;
   `overnight.md` reduced to the queue and one "start here"; stale prose in
   `tools/README.md` and `reference-implementations.md` deleted; `AGENTS.md`
   held under 200 lines by moving the protocol reference to
   `tools/probes/README.md`. *Evidence:* §7. *Verify:* mandatory entry cost
   under 15k tokens; every approximation in §6 findable by one grep.
3. **Hooks for the two rules I keep breaking.** A `PreToolUse` hook that
   refuses `pgrep -f`, `pkill -f`, bare `sleep`, and `until … sleep` loops in
   Bash; a `PreToolUse` hook on `git commit` that requires a gate sentinel
   newer than every tracked file, written only by `tools/gate.sh`. One
   blessed wait idiom (`run_in_background` on the job, `tools/wait_for.sh`
   on its handle) and nothing else in the docs. *Evidence:* §4 items 1 and
   5. *Verify:* the hook rejects the exact commands from the digests.
4. **Verification that reaches the player's layer.** A browser step in the
   gate (extend `debug_smoke.mjs`: button enablement, click → command,
   select → HUD) so `main.ts` is tested at all; the measurements I actually
   make promoted into the protocol or one canonical
   `tools/probes/measure.mts` (rect mean in a stated colour space,
   exact-colour count, edge width, glyph extent against `combined.txt`); a
   `.local/reference/` corpus of DE crops with known scale; a rule that a
   negative `pixels` result cannot be overridden by a picture; the gate's
   output must say "N fidelity tests skipped" rather than pass silently.
   *Evidence:* §5. *Verify:* the five view-layer misses in §5 each have a
   check that would have failed.
5. **Close the fallback leak.** A test that imported rules never read a
   `FALLBACK_RULES` field (or that the importer publishes every field the
   rules consume); fix stone, the onager's projectile speed, drop sites
   (#52), herd/flee ranges; split `rulesFromManifest` into a field table.
   *Verify:* the test fails on `f834ac9` and passes after.
6. **Overnight discipline as structure.** Pre-run Q&A mandatory, answers
   pasted into the queue; `date` printed by the hook every N tool calls and
   read before every progress summary; a stopping rule in every mandate
   ("then continue down the queue" or "then stop"); the morning report
   generated from `git rev-list --count`, `grep -c '\[x\]'` and `gh`; tuning
   items carry a batch budget ("two batches without movement → instrument");
   decoder items run first and alone; concurrent items only in worktrees;
   fixture-clock changes named in the report. *Evidence:* §4 items 2, 4, 7;
   §5 counts.
7. **Delegation, decided.** Retire the general "orchestrate feature
   subagents" recommendation — 189 commits with few corrections were shipped
   inline and the four delegated tasks were all research. Keep two specific
   uses: a fresh-context docs audit at every hand-off (the one Aug 27 audit
   caught 11 stale claims), and worktree-isolated parallel items on
   overnights. *Verify:* hand-off commits shrink the docs rather than grow
   them.
8. **Code hygiene, small.** `Readonly` view of `GameState` at the
   `syncScene`/`updateEntityView` signatures; dedupe `NODE_OF_RESOURCE` and
   `distance`; delete the two dead exports; split or budget the 21.8 s test;
   a `PROTOCOL_VERSION` bump guard; the Sep 17 survey as `tools/survey.py`.
9. **Product decisions for you**, listed rather than taken: the hotkey
   layout (Q/W/G vs B/V/S); starting stone; the wonder's victory; whether
   sessions run one per tree or on branches; whether GPL sources may be
   read for algorithm understanding when the read is recorded; whether
   overnight windows are to be filled or the queue is the stop.

What this plan does not do: add features. The three weeks show the feature
work is fast when the harness holds; every hour lost above was process.
